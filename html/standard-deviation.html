The standard deviation is the square root of the variance: sum up the squared distances of each observation from the mean, average them with n − 1, then take the square root to get back to the data's original unit.

Read aloud: s equals the square root of one over n minus one times the sum from i equals one to n of x sub i minus x bar, squared.

Definitions

sd

Sample standard deviation sss. Pronounced: "s", or "the standard deviation". Often abbreviated SD. The square root   \sqrt{\;}​ over the whole expression undoes the squaring inside the variance: s=s2s = \sqrt{s^2}s=s2​. The result is therefore measured in the data's unit rather than its square, and reads as a "typical" distance of an observation from the mean. For roughly normal data, about 68% of values lie within xˉ±s\bar{x} \pm sxˉ±s and about 95% within xˉ±2s\bar{x} \pm 2sxˉ±2s. The population SD is written σ\sigmaσ ("sigma"). Although s2s^2s2 is unbiased for σ2\sigma^2σ2, sss is slightly biased for σ\sigmaσ (Jensen's inequality).

A single observation xix_ixi​. Pronounced: "x sub i". The iii-th data point.

mean

Sample mean xˉ\bar{x}xˉ. Pronounced: "x bar". The center from which spread is measured. It is the same value for every iii.

square

Squaring, the exponent 222. Pronounced: "squared". Makes every deviation positive so they cannot cancel. The square root outside then brings the unit back.
