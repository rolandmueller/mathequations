Forward Propagation

a(l)=g(W(l) a(l−1)+b(l))\htmlClass{term-out}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\mathbf{a}^{(l)}}} = \htmlClass{term-act}{\textcolor{#10b981}{g}}\big( \htmlClass{term-w}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{W^{(l)}}} \, \htmlClass{term-in}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\mathbf{a}^{(l-1)}}} + \htmlClass{term-b}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{\mathbf{b}^{(l)}}} \big)a(l)=g(W(l)a(l−1)+b(l))

A neural network computes its output layer by layer. Each layer takes the outputs of the previous layer, multiplies them by a weight matrix, adds a bias vector, and passes every entry through the activation function. The result is the layer's output, which feeds the next layer. One matrix multiplication handles all neurons of a layer at once.

Read aloud: a l equals g of W l times a l minus one plus b l.

Definitions

out

Activations of layer lll, a(l)\mathbf{a}^{(l)}a(l). Pronounced: "a l". The superscript (l)(l)(l) is a layer index, not a power. A vector with one entry per node. The last layer's activations are the network's prediction.

act

Activation function ggg. Pronounced: "g". A placeholder for whichever activation the layer uses, and layers can use different ones: typically ReLU, max⁡(0,z)\max(0, z)max(0,z), in hidden layers, and sigmoid or softmax in the output layer, depending on the task (see the Activation Functions page). ReLU and sigmoid are applied entry by entry; softmax uses the whole vector at once. Without an activation, all layers together would collapse into one linear map.

Bias vector b(l)\mathbf{b}^{(l)}b(l). Pronounced: "b l". Shifts each node's threshold. Some hand-built networks leave it out, or add a constant input of 1 instead.
