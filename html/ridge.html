Ridge Regression

Ridge regression chooses the coefficients that balance two goals: fit the data well, measured by the residual sum of squares, and keep the coefficients small, measured by the sum of their squares. The tuning parameter lambda sets the exchange rate between the two. Shrinking coefficients adds a little bias but can cut variance a lot.

Read aloud: minimize over beta the residual sum of squares plus lambda times the sum from j equals one to p of beta j squared.

Definitions

min

Minimization over the coefficients. Pronounced: "minimize over beta". The solution has a closed form and is unique even with more predictors than observations or with collinear predictors, where ordinary least squares breaks down.

rss

Residual sum of squares. Pronounced: "the residual sum of squares". The ordinary least squares loss. On its own, minimizing it gives the OLS coefficients.

lambda

Tuning parameter λ≥0\lambda \ge 0λ≥0. Pronounced: "lambda". λ=0\lambda = 0λ=0 gives OLS; λ→∞\lambda \to \inftyλ→∞ shrinks every slope toward 0. Choose it by cross-validation (RidgeCV\texttt{RidgeCV}RidgeCV). scikit-learn calls it alpha\texttt{alpha}alpha. In neural networks the same penalty is called weight decay (kernel_regularizer=regularizers.l2(...)\texttt{kernel\_regularizer=regularizers.l2(...)}kernel_regularizer=regularizers.l2(...) in keras).

penalty

L2 penalty ∑j=1pβj2\sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j^2∑j=1p​βj2​. Pronounced: "the sum from j equals one to p of beta j squared". Also written ∥β∥22\lVert \beta \rVert_2^2∥β∥22​, "the squared L2 norm of beta". Shrinks all coefficients smoothly toward zero but never exactly to zero, so every predictor stays in the model. The intercept β0\beta_0β0​ is not penalized. Because the penalty compares coefficient sizes, standardize the predictors first; otherwise the units decide which coefficients get shrunk.
