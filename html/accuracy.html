Accuracy

Accuracy=TP+TNTP+TN+FP+FN\htmlClass{term-acc}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\text{Accuracy}}} = \frac{\htmlClass{term-tp}{\textcolor{#10b981}{TP}} + \htmlClass{term-tn}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{TN}}}{\htmlClass{term-tp}{\textcolor{#10b981}{TP}} + \htmlClass{term-tn}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{TN}} + \htmlClass{term-fp}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{FP}} + \htmlClass{term-fn}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{FN}}}Accuracy=TP+TN+FP+FNTP+TN​

Accuracy is the share of predictions that are right. The four cells of the confusion matrix sort every prediction: true positives and true negatives are correct, while false positives and false negatives are the two kinds of mistakes.

Read aloud: accuracy equals T P plus T N, over the total of T P, T N, F P and F N.

Definitions

acc

Accuracy. Pronounced: "accuracy". The fraction of correct predictions, between 0 and 1. The error rate (misclassification rate) is 1−Accuracy1 - \text{Accuracy}1−Accuracy; it is the average of the 0-1 loss, which charges 1 for every mistake and 0 otherwise. Accuracy misleads when classes are imbalanced: if 99% of emails are not spam, always predicting "not spam" scores 99% while catching nothing. It also treats both error types as equally costly, which they rarely are. A loss function with different costs for false positives and false negatives fixes that.

tp

True positives TPTPTP. Pronounced: "T P", or "true positives". Cases predicted positive that really are positive. Example: a sick patient whose test says "sick".

tn

True negatives TNTNTN. Pronounced: "T N", or "true negatives". Cases predicted negative that really are negative.

fp

False positives FPFPFP. Pronounced: "F P", or "false positives". Also called Type I errors or false alarms. Cases predicted positive that are actually negative. Example: a healthy patient told they are sick.

fn

False negatives FNFNFN. Pronounced: "F N", or "false negatives". Also called Type II errors or misses. Cases predicted negative that are actually positive. Example: a sick patient sent home as healthy.
