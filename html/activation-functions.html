The activation function g turns a neuron's weighted sum into its output, and is what makes a network nonlinear. Three are used almost everywhere. ReLU takes the larger of zero and its input: it passes positive values through and cuts negative ones off. The sigmoid squashes any number into the range 0 to 1 with an exponential. Softmax works on a whole layer at once: it turns the score of class k into a probability by comparing it with the sum over all classes of the exponentiated scores.

Read aloud: ReLU of z equals the maximum of zero and z. Sigma of z equals one over one plus e to the minus z. Softmax of z, component k, equals e to the z k, over the sum from l equals one to K of e to the z l.

Definitions

relu

Rectified linear unit ReLU(z)\mathrm{ReLU}(z)ReLU(z). Pronounced: "ReLU of z" ("ray-loo"), or "the rectified linear unit". The default for hidden layers: layers.Dense(64, activation="relu")\texttt{layers.Dense(64, activation="relu")}layers.Dense(64, activation="relu") in keras. Cheap to compute, and its slope is exactly 1 for every positive input, so gradients do not shrink as they pass back through many layers (unlike the sigmoid). Many ReLUs together build any shape out of straight pieces. Its slope is 0 for negative inputs, so a neuron whose input is always negative stops learning ("dying ReLU"). Leaky ReLU, max⁡(0.01z,z)\max(0.01z, z)max(0.01z,z), keeps a small slope there.

Score of class kkk, zkz_kzk​. Pronounced: "z k". The pre-activation of output node kkk, often called a logit. Only differences between scores matter: adding the same constant to all of them leaves the probabilities unchanged.
