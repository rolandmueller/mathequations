Log-Linear Regression

log⁡(Y)=β0+β1X+ε\htmlClass{term-logy}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\log(Y)}} = \htmlClass{term-b0}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\beta_0}} + \htmlClass{term-b1}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\beta_1}} \htmlClass{term-x}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{X}} + \htmlClass{term-eps}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{\varepsilon}}log(Y)=β0​+β1​X+ε

Taking the logarithm of the response turns multiplicative relationships into additive ones. Now the slope describes a percentage change: each extra unit of the predictor multiplies Y by a constant factor. The intercept and error term also live on the log scale.

Read aloud: log of Y equals beta zero plus beta one times X plus epsilon.
