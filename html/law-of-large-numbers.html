Law of Large Numbers

Xˉn=1n∑i=1nXi    →  n→∞      μ\htmlClass{term-mean}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\bar{X}_n}} = \htmlClass{term-norm}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\frac{1}{n}}} \htmlClass{term-sum}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}}} \htmlClass{term-draw}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{X_i}} \;\; \htmlClass{term-conv}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{\xrightarrow{\; n \to \infty \;}}} \;\; \htmlClass{term-mu}{\textcolor{#06b6d4}{\mu}}Xˉn​=n1​i=1∑n​Xi​n→∞​μ

The average of many independent draws, that is the sum of the individual draws divided by their number, settles down to the true expected value as the number of draws grows. Toss a die 10 times and the average may be 4.2; toss it 100000 times and it will be very close to 3.5. This is why simulation works: averaging many random draws recovers probabilities and expectations.

Read aloud: x bar n, which is one over n times the sum from i equals one to n of X i, converges as n goes to infinity to mu.
