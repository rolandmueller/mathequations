Regression Tree

A regression tree chops the predictor space into boxes and predicts a constant in each. Its residual sum of squares goes through every box, and within it every training point, comparing the actual response with the box's prediction, the mean of the responses in that box. Splits are chosen to make this as small as possible.

Read aloud: R S S equals the sum from j equals one to J of the sum over i in R j of y sub i minus y hat R j, squared.

Definitions

rss

Residual sum of squares RSS\mathrm{RSS}RSS. Pronounced: "R S S". Finding the best partition is computationally infeasible, so trees are grown greedily by recursive binary splitting: at each step, take the single split that reduces RSS the most, without looking ahead. Deep trees overfit. Cost-complexity pruning adds α∣T∣\alpha |T|α∣T∣ ("alpha times the number of leaves") to the RSS and chooses α\alphaα by cross-validation (ccp_alpha\texttt{ccp\_alpha}ccp_alpha in scikit-learn). Simpler stopping rules are max_depth\texttt{max\_depth}max_depth and min_samples_leaf\texttt{min\_samples\_leaf}min_samples_leaf.

Trees are easy to explain and handle interactions and nonlinearity naturally, but a single tree is unstable: a small change in the data can produce a very different tree. Bagging and random forests fix that.
