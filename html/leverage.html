Leverage

Some points pull on the regression line more than others. The leverage of an observation is a baseline share that every point gets, plus its squared distance from the center of the predictor relative to the total spread of the predictor. Points with unusual x values can tilt the whole line on their own.

Read aloud: h i equals one over n plus x i minus x bar, squared, over the sum of all squared deviations of x.
