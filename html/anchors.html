Anchors (Scoped Rules)

An anchor explains a prediction with an IF-THEN rule, such as "IF age < 25 AND no prior defaults THEN approve". The rule's precision is the average over perturbed samples that satisfy the rule of whether the model's prediction stays the same. A rule qualifies as an anchor if this is at least a threshold tau, e.g. 95%: as long as the anchor holds, the other features hardly matter.

Read aloud: the precision of A equals the expected value over z drawn near x satisfying A of the indicator that f of x equals f of z, which must be at least tau.

Definitions

prec

Precision of rule AAA. Pronounced: "the precision of A". Not the classification metric of the same name, though the idea is similar: how often the rule's "prediction" is right. Anchors are searched for to have high precision and, among those, high coverage (the share of data the rule applies to). Implementations: alibi\texttt{alibi}alibi (AnchorTabular\texttt{AnchorTabular}AnchorTabular), anchor-exp\texttt{anchor-exp}anchor-exp. Compared with LIME, anchors state explicitly where the explanation applies.

expect

Expectation over the perturbation distribution EDx(z∣A)\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_x(z \mid A)}EDx​(z∣A)​. Pronounced: "the expected value over z drawn near x satisfying A". zzz are perturbed versions of the instance xxx in which the anchor's conditions stay fixed and all other features vary. Estimated by sampling.

agree

Agreement indicator If(x)=f(z)\mathbb{I}_{f(x) = f(z)}If(x)=f(z)​. Pronounced: "the indicator that f of x equals f of z". 1 if the black box fff predicts the same class for zzz as for xxx.

tau

Precision threshold τ\tauτ. Pronounced: "tau". Chosen by the user, e.g. 0.95. Higher thresholds give longer, more specific rules.
