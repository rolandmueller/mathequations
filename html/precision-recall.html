Three rates from the confusion matrix. Precision: of everything flagged positive, how much really is? Recall: of all real positives, how many did we catch? False positive rate: of all real negatives, how many did we wrongly flag? The counts are true positives, false positives, false negatives and true negatives. The ROC curve plots recall against the false positive rate as the classification threshold moves from strict to lenient.

Read aloud: precision equals T P over T P plus F P. Recall, the true positive rate, equals T P over T P plus F N. The false positive rate equals F P over F P plus T N.

Definitions

precision

Precision, or positive predictive value. Pronounced: "precision". Matters when false alarms are expensive (spam filters, fraud alerts that freeze accounts). Unlike recall and FPR, it depends on how common positives are: the same test has lower precision in a population where the condition is rare. The F1 score combines precision and recall as their harmonic mean: F1=2⋅Precision⋅RecallPrecision+RecallF_1 = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}F1​=Precision+Recall2⋅Precision⋅Recall​.

recall

Recall, sensitivity, or true positive rate (TPR). Pronounced: "recall", or "T P R". Matters when misses are expensive (cancer screening). Predicting everything as positive gives recall 1, so it must always be reported alongside precision or FPR.

fpr

False positive rate (FPR). Pronounced: "F P R". Equals 1−specificity1 - \text{specificity}1−specificity, where specificity =TNTN+FP= \frac{TN}{TN + FP}=TN+FPTN​ is the true negative rate. The fourth rate in ISLR's Table 4.6 is the negative predictive value, TNTN+FN\frac{TN}{TN + FN}TN+FNTN​. The ROC curve runs from (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0) (flag nothing) to (1,1)(1, 1)(1,1) (flag everything). The area under it (AUC) is the probability that a random positive gets a higher score than a random negative: 0.5 is guessing, 1 is perfect. sklearn.metrics.roc_curve\texttt{sklearn.metrics.roc\_curve}sklearn.metrics.roc_curve, roc_auc_score\texttt{roc\_auc\_score}roc_auc_score. For multiclass problems, compute these per class (one-vs-rest) and average: "macro" weights classes equally, "micro" weights observations equally.
