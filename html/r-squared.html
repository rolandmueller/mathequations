Coefficient of Determination

R2=1−RSSTSS=1−∑i(yi−y^i)2∑i(yi−yˉ)2\htmlClass{term-r2}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{R^2}} = 1 - \frac{\htmlClass{term-rss}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\mathrm{RSS}}}}{\htmlClass{term-tss}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\mathrm{TSS}}}} = 1 - \frac{\htmlClass{term-rss}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\sum_i (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}}}{\htmlClass{term-tss}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\sum_i (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}}R2=1−TSSRSS​=1−∑i​(yi​−yˉ​)2∑i​(yi​−y^​i​)2​

R squared measures how much of the variation in the response a model explains. The total sum of squares is the error of the dumbest model, which always predicts the mean. The residual sum of squares is the error left after fitting. One minus their ratio is the share of variance the model removes.

Read aloud: R squared equals one minus R S S over T S S.

Definitions

r2

Coefficient of determination R2R^2R2. Pronounced: "R squared". Between 0 and 1 on the training data. In simple linear regression R2=r2R^2 = r^2R2=r2, the squared correlation. It never decreases when a predictor is added, even a useless one, so on its own it rewards overfitting; adjusted R2R^2R2 or test-set R2R^2R2 correct for this. On new data it can be negative, meaning the model is worse than predicting the mean. A low R2R^2R2 does not mean a coefficient is unimportant, and a high R2R^2R2 does not mean the model is causal.

rss

Residual sum of squares RSS\mathrm{RSS}RSS. Pronounced: "R S S". Squared gaps between observed yiy_iyi​ and fitted y^i\hat{y}_iy^​i​ ("y hat i"). What the model fails to explain.

tss

Total sum of squares TSS\mathrm{TSS}TSS. Pronounced: "T S S". Squared gaps between yiy_iyi​ and the mean yˉ\bar{y}yˉ​. It equals (n−1) sy2(n-1)\,s_y^2(n−1)sy2​, the total variation before any model is fitted.
