Population Variance

σ2=1N∑i=1N(xi−μ)2\htmlClass{term-popvar}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\sigma^2}} = \htmlClass{term-norm}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\frac{1}{N}}} \htmlClass{term-sum}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}}} \left( \htmlClass{term-value}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{x_i}} - \htmlClass{term-mu}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{\mu}} \right)^{\htmlClass{term-square}{\textcolor{#06b6d4}{2}}}σ2=N1​i=1∑N​(xi​−μ)2

The population variance measures the spread of an entire population, not a sample. Take every value in the population, find its distance from the population mean, square it, add them all up, and divide by the population size N. No correction is needed, because the true mean is known rather than estimated.

Read aloud: sigma squared equals one over N times the sum from i equals one to N of x sub i minus mu, squared.
