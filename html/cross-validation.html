k-Fold Cross-Validation

Cross-validation estimates the test error without a separate test set. Split the data into k folds; for each fold, train on the other k − 1 folds and compute the error on the held-out fold. Then average the k errors. Every observation is used for testing exactly once.

Read aloud: C V sub k equals one over k times the sum from i equals one to k of M S E sub i.

Definitions

cv

Cross-validation estimate CV(k)\mathrm{CV}_{(k)}CV(k)​. Pronounced: "C V sub k". Used to compare models and to choose tuning parameters such as polynomial degree, KKK in KNN, or λ\lambdaλ in ridge and lasso: pick the setting with the lowest CV error. sklearn.model_selection.cross_val_score(model, X, y, cv=5)\texttt{sklearn.model\_selection.cross\_val\_score(model, X, y, cv=5)}sklearn.model_selection.cross_val_score(model, X, y, cv=5). Any preprocessing that learns from data (scaling, feature selection) must happen inside each fold, otherwise information leaks from the held-out fold. A Pipeline\texttt{Pipeline}Pipeline takes care of this.

Summation over folds ∑i=1k\sum_{i=1}^{k}∑i=1k​. Pronounced: "the sum from i equals one to k". Here iii counts folds, not observations.

fold

Held-out error MSEi\mathrm{MSE}_iMSEi​. Pronounced: "M S E sub i". The mean squared error on fold iii of a model trained without fold iii. For classification, use the error rate or another metric instead. The spread of the kkk values hints at how uncertain the CV estimate is.
