A z-score tells you how unusual an observation is: its distance from the mean, expressed in units of standard deviations. Standardized variables have mean 0 and standard deviation 1, which makes values on different scales comparable.

Read aloud: z sub i equals x sub i minus x bar, over s.

Definitions

zscore

Standardized score ziz_izi​. Pronounced: "z sub i", or "the z-score of observation i". The letter is "zee" in American English and "zed" in British English. The subscript iii matches the one on xix_ixi​: every observation gets its own z-score, so there are nnn of them. The score is dimensionless. z=2z = 2z=2 means "two standard deviations above the mean", whatever the original unit. Standardization is linear, so it does not change the shape of the distribution: skewed data stay skewed. Tail probabilities from the normal table only apply if the data are approximately normal.

value

A single observation xix_ixi​. Pronounced: "x sub i". The iii-th data point, in its original unit. iii can be any of 1,…,n1, \dots, n1,…,n.

mean

Sample mean xˉ\bar{x}xˉ. Pronounced: "x bar". Subtracting it centers the data at zero. It has no subscript because it is the same for every observation.

sd

Sample standard deviation sss. Pronounced: "s". Dividing by it rescales the data to unit spread and cancels the unit.
