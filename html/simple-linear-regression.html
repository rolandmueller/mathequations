Simple Linear Regression

Y=β0+β1X+ε\htmlClass{term-y}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{Y}} = \htmlClass{term-b0}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\beta_0}} + \htmlClass{term-b1}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\beta_1}} \htmlClass{term-x}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{X}} + \htmlClass{term-eps}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{\varepsilon}}Y=β0​+β1​X+ε

Linear regression models a response as a straight-line function of a predictor: a starting value plus a slope times the predictor, plus an error term for everything the line does not capture.

Read aloud: Y equals beta zero plus beta one times X plus epsilon.

Definitions

y

Response YYY. Pronounced: "Y". Also called the outcome, target, or dependent variable. The quantity we want to predict or explain, e.g. sales.

b0

Intercept β0\beta_0β0​. Pronounced: "beta zero", or "beta naught". The expected value of YYY when X=0X = 0X=0. Often not meaningful by itself (nobody has height 0), but needed to place the line.

b1

Slope β1\beta_1β1​. Pronounced: "beta one". The expected change in YYY for a one-unit increase in XXX. It describes association; reading it as "the effect of XXX" requires that no confounder drives both. Estimated by least squares as β^1\hat{\beta}_1β^​1​.

x

Predictor XXX. Pronounced: "X". Also called the feature, input, or independent variable. E.g. TV advertising budget.

eps

Error term ε\varepsilonε. Pronounced: "epsilon". Everything the line misses: omitted variables, measurement error, randomness. Standard inference assumes errors are independent with mean 0 and constant variance σ2\sigma^2σ2. The observed estimates of ε\varepsilonε are the residuals yi−y^iy_i - \hat{y}_iyi​−y^​i​.
