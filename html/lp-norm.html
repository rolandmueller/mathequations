Lp Norm of a Vector

∥x∥p=(∑i=1n∣xi∣p)1/p\htmlClass{term-norm}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_p}} = \Big( \htmlClass{term-sum}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}}} \htmlClass{term-abs}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{|x_i|}}^{\htmlClass{term-p}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{p}}} \Big)^{\htmlClass{term-root}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{1/p}}}∥x∥p​=(i=1∑n​∣xi​∣p)1/p

A norm measures the size, or length, of a vector. The Lp norm takes the absolute value of each component, raises it to the power p, adds them up, and then takes the p-th root to undo the power. The choice of p decides how strongly large components count: p = 1 adds the components up, p = 2 is the ordinary straight-line length, and the larger p gets, the more the largest component dominates.

Read aloud: the L p norm of x equals the sum from i equals one to n of the absolute value of x i to the p, all to the power one over p.
