Gini Index

Gm=∑k=1Kp^mk(1−p^mk)\htmlClass{term-gini}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{G_m}} = \htmlClass{term-sum}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}}} \htmlClass{term-pk}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\hat{p}_{mk}}} \htmlClass{term-qk}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\left( 1 - \hat{p}_{mk} \right)}}Gm​=k=1∑K​p^​mk​(1−p^​mk​)

A classification tree splits the data so that each leaf is as pure as possible. The Gini index measures impurity: for each class, multiply the share of that class in the node by the share of everything else. A node containing only one class scores 0.

Read aloud: G sub m equals the sum from k equals one to K of p hat m k times one minus p hat m k.

gini

Gini index of node mmm, GmG_mGm​. Pronounced: "G sub m", or "the Gini impurity of node m". At each step the tree tries every predictor and cut point and picks the split that most reduces the size-weighted impurity of the two child nodes. The maximum for two classes is 0.5, at a 50/50 mix.
