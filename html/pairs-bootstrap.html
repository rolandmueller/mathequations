Pairs Bootstrap for Regression

(xi∗,yi∗)∼{(x1,y1),…,(xn,yn)}CI95%(β1)=[ β^1,(2.5%)∗,  β^1,(97.5%)∗ ]\begin{aligned} \htmlClass{term-resample}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{(x_i^{*}, y_i^{*})}} &\sim \htmlClass{term-pairs}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\{ (x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_n, y_n) \}}} \\[8pt] \htmlClass{term-ci}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}(\beta_1)}} &= \Big[ \, \htmlClass{term-lower}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\hat{\beta}^{*}_{1,(2.5\%)}}} , \; \htmlClass{term-upper}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{\hat{\beta}^{*}_{1,(97.5\%)}}} \, \Big] \end{aligned}(xi∗​,yi∗​)CI95%​(β1​)​∼{(x1​,y1​),…,(xn​,yn​)}=[β^​1,(2.5%)∗​,β^​1,(97.5%)∗​]​

How uncertain is a fitted slope? Resample whole (x, y) pairs with replacement from the observed pairs, keeping each x with its own y, refit the line, and record the slope. Repeating this thousands of times gives a distribution of slopes, and its 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles form a 95% confidence interval for the slope.

Read aloud: x i star, y i star is drawn from the observed pairs. The 95 percent confidence interval for beta one runs from the 2.5th percentile of the bootstrap slopes to the 97.5th percentile.

Definitions

resample

Resampled pair (xi∗,yi∗)(x_i^{*}, y_i^{*})(xi∗​,yi∗​). Pronounced: "x i star, y i star". Resample indices, not values, so the pairs stay intact: inds = np.random.choice(np.arange(n), size=n)\texttt{inds = np.random.choice(np.arange(n), size=n)}inds = np.random.choice(np.arange(n), size=n), then slope, intercept = np.polyfit(x[inds], y[inds], 1)\texttt{slope, intercept = np.polyfit(x[inds], y[inds], 1)}slope, intercept = np.polyfit(x[inds], y[inds], 1).

pairs

Observed pairs. Pronounced: "the observed pairs". Each row of the data is one case. Shuffling x and y separately would destroy the relationship; that is what a permutation test does on purpose, not a bootstrap.

ci

Confidence interval for the slope. Pronounced: "the 95 percent confidence interval for beta one". Compare it with the formula interval β^1±2 SE(β^1)\hat{\beta}_1 \pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}(\hat{\beta}_1)β^​1​±2SE(β^​1​) from ISLR 3.1.2. The pairs bootstrap does not assume constant error variance, so it stays honest when the scatter fans out. Plotting the bootstrapped lines on top of the data shows the uncertainty directly.

lower

Lower bound. Pronounced: "the 2.5th percentile of the bootstrap slopes".

upper

Upper bound. Pronounced: "the 97.5th percentile of the bootstrap slopes". The same replicates also give intervals for the intercept or for a prediction at a given x.
