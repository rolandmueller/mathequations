The same two methods, seen geometrically. Find the coefficients with the smallest residual sum of squares, but only among those whose size stays within a budget. The lasso measures size by the sum of absolute values, a diamond-shaped region; ridge by the sum of squares, a circle. The best fit usually touches a corner of the diamond, where some coefficients are exactly zero; a circle has no corners.

Read aloud: minimize over beta the R S S subject to the sum of absolute values of beta j, or for ridge the sum of beta j squared, being at most s.

Definitions

min

Minimization over the coefficients. Pronounced: "minimize over beta".

rss

Residual sum of squares RSS\mathrm{RSS}RSS. Pronounced: "the R S S". Its contours are ellipses around the unconstrained least squares solution. The constrained solution is where the smallest ellipse touches the allowed region (ISLR 6.2.2, Figure 6.7).

st

"Subject to". Pronounced: "subject to". Turns the penalty form ("RSS plus lambda times penalty") into a hard limit. For every λ\lambdaλ there is a budget sss that gives the same solution, and vice versa.

l1

L1 norm ∑j=1p∣βj∣\sum_{j=1}^{p} |\beta_j|∑j=1p​∣βj​∣. Pronounced: "the sum of absolute values of beta j". In two dimensions, ∣β1∣+∣β2∣≤s|\beta_1| + |\beta_2| \le s∣β1​∣+∣β2​∣≤s is a diamond with corners on the axes. Corners mean sparse solutions, which is why the lasso selects variables.

l2

Squared L2 norm ∑j=1pβj2\sum_{j=1}^{p} \beta_j^2∑j=1p​βj2​. Pronounced: "the sum of beta j squared". β12+β22≤s\beta_1^2 + \beta_2^2 \le sβ12​+β22​≤s is a disk: coefficients shrink smoothly but almost never hit exactly zero.

budget

Budget sss. Pronounced: "s". A large sss includes the least squares solution and changes nothing; a small sss forces strong shrinkage. The explained.ai article on regularization animates exactly this picture.
