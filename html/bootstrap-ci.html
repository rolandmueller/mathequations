Bootstrap Percentile Confidence Interval

CI95%=[ θ^(2.5%)∗,  θ^(97.5%)∗ ]\htmlClass{term-ci}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}}} = \Big[ \, \htmlClass{term-lower}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\hat{\theta}^{*}_{(2.5\%)}}} , \; \htmlClass{term-upper}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\hat{\theta}^{*}_{(97.5\%)}}} \, \Big]CI95%​=[θ^(2.5%)∗​,θ^(97.5%)∗​]

The bootstrap 95% confidence interval needs no formula for the standard error. Draw many bootstrap resamples, compute the statistic on each, and cut off the extreme 2.5% on both sides: the interval runs from the 2.5th percentile to the 97.5th percentile of the bootstrap replicates.

Read aloud: the 95 percent confidence interval runs from the 2.5th percentile of theta hat star to the 97.5th percentile of theta hat star.

Bootstrap percentile interval CI95%\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}CI95%​. Pronounced: "the ninety-five percent confidence interval". Recipe in numpy: build replicates with np.mean(np.random.choice(data, size=len(data)))\texttt{np.mean(np.random.choice(data, size=len(data)))}np.mean(np.random.choice(data, size=len(data))) repeated 10000 times, then take np.percentile(bs_reps, [2.5, 97.5])\texttt{np.percentile(bs\_reps, [2.5, 97.5])}np.percentile(bs_reps, [2.5, 97.5]). Works for any statistic: mean, median, standard deviation, a rate, a regression slope. It assumes the sample represents the population and observations are independent; small samples give intervals that are too narrow.

Their spread approximates the sampling distribution of θ^\hat{\theta}θ^; their standard deviation is the bootstrap standard error.
