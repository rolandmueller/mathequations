F-Statistic for Regression

F=(TSS−RSS) / pRSS / (n−p−1)\htmlClass{term-f}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{F}} = \frac{\htmlClass{term-explained}{\textcolor{#10b981}{(\mathrm{TSS} - \mathrm{RSS})}} \, / \, \htmlClass{term-p}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{p}}}{\htmlClass{term-rss}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\mathrm{RSS}}} \, / \, \htmlClass{term-df}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{(n - p - 1)}}}F=RSS/(n−p−1)(TSS−RSS)/p​

Does the model as a whole explain anything? The F-statistic compares the variation the model explains, per predictor, with the variation it leaves unexplained, per remaining degree of freedom. If no predictor matters, both are estimates of the same noise and F is near 1; a large F says at least one predictor is related to the response.

Read aloud: F equals T S S minus R S S over p, divided by R S S over n minus p minus one.
