Central Limit Theorem

Xˉn  ∼approx.  N ⁣(μ,  σ2n)\htmlClass{term-mean}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\bar{X}_n}} \; \htmlClass{term-approx}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\overset{\text{approx.}}{\sim}}} \; \htmlClass{term-normal}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\mathcal{N}}}\!\left( \htmlClass{term-mu}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\mu}}, \; \frac{\htmlClass{term-var}{\textcolor{#ef4444}{\sigma^2}}}{\htmlClass{term-n}{\textcolor{#06b6d4}{n}}} \right)Xˉn​∼approx.N(μ,nσ2​)

Whatever the shape of the data, the mean of a sample is approximately normally distributed once the sample is large enough. That sampling distribution is centered on the true mean, and its variance is the population variance divided by the sample size. This is the source of the square-root-of-n law and of most textbook confidence intervals.

Read aloud: x bar n is approximately distributed as a normal with mean mu and variance sigma squared over n.

Definitions

mean

Sample mean Xˉn\bar{X}_nXˉn​. Pronounced: "x bar n". Capital XXX because it is a random variable: it would come out differently for each new sample. Simulate it: draw many samples, compute each mean, and look at the histogram or ECDF of the means.

approx

"Is approximately distributed as". Pronounced: "is approximately distributed as". How large nnn must be depends on the data. For roughly symmetric data, n≥30n \ge 30n≥30 is the usual rule of thumb (ISRS's "normality condition"); strongly skewed or heavy-tailed data need far more. Observations must be independent.

normal

Normal distribution N\mathcal{N}N. Pronounced: "a normal", or "N".

mu

Population mean μ\muμ. Pronounced: "mu". Sample means are unbiased: they scatter around μ\muμ, not around some other value.

var

Population variance σ2\sigma^2σ2. Pronounced: "sigma squared". The spread of individual observations. The CLT requires it to be finite.

n

Sample size nnn. Pronounced: "n". The standard deviation of the sampling distribution is σ/n\sigma / \sqrt{n}σ/n​, the standard error. Four times the data halves the uncertainty.
