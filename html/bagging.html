Bagging and Random Forests

f^bag(x)=1B∑b=1Bf^∗b(x)\htmlClass{term-bag}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{\hat{f}_{\text{bag}}(x)}} = \htmlClass{term-norm}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\frac{1}{B}}} \htmlClass{term-sum}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\sum_{b=1}^{B}}} \htmlClass{term-tree}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\hat{f}^{*b}(x)}}f^​bag​(x)=B1​b=1∑B​f^​∗b(x)

Bagging (bootstrap aggregating) tames unstable models. Train a deep tree on each of B bootstrap resamples, then predict by averaging the predictions of all the trees. Each individual tree overfits in its own way, and averaging cancels much of that variance.

Read aloud: f hat bag of x equals one over B times the sum from b equals one to B of f hat star b of x.

Definitions

bag

Bagged prediction f^bag(x)\hat{f}_{\text{bag}}(x)f^​bag​(x). Pronounced: "f hat bag of x". For classification, use a majority vote or average the predicted probabilities. Each tree leaves out about a third of the observations; predicting each observation with only the trees that did not see it gives the out-of-bag (OOB) error, a free estimate of the test error (oob_score=True\texttt{oob\_score=True}oob_score=True).

norm

Normalization 1B\frac{1}{B}B1​. Pronounced: "one over B". BBB is the number of trees. More trees do not cause overfitting; past a few hundred they just stop helping.

sum

Summation over trees ∑b=1B\sum_{b=1}^{B}∑b=1B​. Pronounced: "the sum from b equals one to B".

tree

Tree fitted to bootstrap sample bbb, f^∗b(x)\hat{f}^{*b}(x)f^​∗b(x). Pronounced: "f hat star b of x". Averaging BBB predictions with variance σ2\sigma^2σ2 and pairwise correlation ρ\rhoρ gives variance ρ σ2+1−ρB σ2\rho\,\sigma^2 + \frac{1 - \rho}{B}\,\sigma^2ρσ2+B1−ρ​σ2. More trees shrink the second term, but only decorrelating the trees shrinks the first. A random forest does this by letting each split consider only a random subset of mmm predictors (often m≈pm \approx \sqrt{p}m≈p​ for classification, max_features\texttt{max\_features}max_features), so one strong predictor cannot dominate every tree.
