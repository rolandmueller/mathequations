Median Absolute Deviation

The median absolute deviation is the robust cousin of the standard deviation. Measure how far each observation lies from the median, ignore the sign, and take the median of those distances. Medians instead of means make it almost immune to outliers.

Read aloud: M A D equals the median over i of the absolute value of x sub i minus x tilde.

Definitions

mad

Median absolute deviation MAD\mathrm{MAD}MAD. Pronounced: "M A D", or "mad". Up to half of the data can be wildly wrong before the MAD breaks down; for the standard deviation, a single bad value is enough. For normal data σ≈1.4826⋅MAD\sigma \approx 1.4826 \cdot \mathrm{MAD}σ≈1.4826⋅MAD, so that scaled version is used as a robust stand-in for the SD (scipy.stats.median_abs_deviation(x, scale="normal")\texttt{scipy.stats.median\_abs\_deviation(x, scale="normal")}scipy.stats.median_abs_deviation(x, scale="normal")). Outer median median⁡i\operatorname{median}_imediani​. Pronounced: "the median over i". Takes the middle value of the nnn absolute deviations. The running index iii goes through all observations 1,…,n1, \dots, n1,…,n.

Sample median x~\tilde{x}x~. Pronounced: "x tilde". The middle value of the data: half the observations lie below it, half above. It is the center from which deviations are measured, chosen instead of xˉ\bar{x}xˉ for robustness.
