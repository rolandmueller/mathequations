Benford's Law

P(d)=log⁡10(1+1d)\htmlClass{term-p}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{P(d)}} = \htmlClass{term-log}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\log_{10}}}\left( \htmlClass{term-one}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{1}} + \htmlClass{term-recip}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\frac{1}{d}}} \right)P(d)=log10​(1+d1​)

In many real-world datasets (populations, river lengths, invoices, stock prices) the probability that a number starts with digit d is far from uniform: about 30% start with 1 and fewer than 5% with 9. The rule is the base-10 logarithm of one plus one over d.

Read aloud: P of d equals log base ten of one plus one over d.
