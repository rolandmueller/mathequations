Confidence Interval for a Difference of Means

The same recipe as for one mean, point estimate plus or minus t star times standard error, applied to a difference. The interval is centered on the observed difference between the mean of group 1 and the mean of group 2. Its half-width is a critical value times the standard error of the difference, which adds up the uncertainty of both group means.

Read aloud: the confidence interval is x bar one minus x bar two, plus or minus t star with df degrees of freedom times the square root of s one squared over n one plus s two squared over n two.

Definitions

ci

Confidence interval for μ1−μ2\mu_1 - \mu_2μ1​−μ2​. Pronounced: "the confidence interval for mu one minus mu two". Gives the size of the difference along with its uncertainty, which a p-value alone does not. A 95% interval that excludes 0 corresponds to rejecting H0:μ1=μ2H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2H0​:μ1​=μ2​ at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05 in a two-sided test. Conditions: independence within and between groups, and each group roughly normal or large enough. Paired data (the same subjects measured twice) need a one-sample interval on the differences instead.

mean1

Mean of group 1, xˉ1\bar{x}_1xˉ1​. Pronounced: "x bar one".

mean2

Mean of group 2, xˉ2\bar{x}_2xˉ2​. Pronounced: "x bar two".

crit

Critical value tdf⋆t^{\star}_{df}tdf⋆​. Pronounced: "t star with df degrees of freedom". For a 95% interval, the 97.5th percentile of the ttt distribution. For hand calculations ISRS uses the conservative df=min⁡(n1−1,n2−1)df = \min(n_1 - 1, n_2 - 1)df=min(n1​−1,n2​−1); software uses the Welch–Satterthwaite dfdfdf, which is larger and gives a slightly narrower interval.

se

Standard error of the difference. Pronounced: "the square root of s one squared over n one plus s two squared over n two". Variances of independent estimates add, so the uncertainty of a difference is larger than that of either mean alone. It is the same SE as in the denominator of Welch's ttt statistic.
