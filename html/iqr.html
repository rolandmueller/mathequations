Interquartile Range and Boxplot Fences

The interquartile range is the width of the middle half of the data: from the first quartile to the third quartile. It is the box in a boxplot. The whiskers reach out at most 1.5 box-widths beyond the box; points outside these fences are drawn individually as potential outliers.

Read aloud: I Q R equals Q three minus Q one. The fences run from Q one minus one point five times I Q R to Q three plus one point five times I Q R.

Definitions

iqr

Interquartile range IQR\mathrm{IQR}IQR. Pronounced: "I Q R", or "the interquartile range". A robust measure of spread: it ignores the lowest and highest 25% of values, so a single extreme outlier cannot change it. For normally distributed data, IQR≈1.35 σ\mathrm{IQR} \approx 1.35\,\sigmaIQR≈1.35σ. In pandas: df.x.quantile(0.75) - df.x.quantile(0.25)\texttt{df.x.quantile(0.75) - df.x.quantile(0.25)}df.x.quantile(0.75) - df.x.quantile(0.25).

q1

First quartile Q1Q_1Q1​. Pronounced: "Q one". Also the 25th percentile. A quarter of the observations lie below it. There are several conventions for interpolating between data points, so different software can give slightly different values on small samples.

q3

Third quartile Q3Q_3Q3​. Pronounced: "Q three". Also the 75th percentile. Three quarters of the observations lie below it. The median is Q2Q_2Q2​.

fence

Tukey's fences. Pronounced: "the fences", or "the whisker limits". Values outside this interval are flagged as potential outliers. "Potential" matters: in heavy-tailed or skewed data many genuine values fall outside, so a flagged point is a prompt to look, not a reason to delete.

k

Tukey's constant 1.51.51.5. Pronounced: "one point five". A convention from John Tukey, not a law of nature. For normal data, about 0.7% of observations fall outside the fences. Using 3 instead of 1.5 flags only "far out" points.
