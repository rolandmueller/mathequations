Conditional Probability

P(A∣B)=P(A∩B)P(B)\htmlClass{term-cond}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{P(A \mid B)}} = \frac{\htmlClass{term-joint}{\textcolor{#10b981}{P(A \cap B)}}}{\htmlClass{term-given}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{P(B)}}}P(A∣B)=P(B)P(A∩B)​

The probability of A once we know B happened is the share of cases where both happen, measured only within the cases where B happens. Knowing B shrinks the world to the B-bucket; we then ask how often A occurs inside it. This is exactly what a pandas groupby followed by a mean of a 0/1 column computes.

Read aloud: the probability of A given B equals the probability of A and B over the probability of B.

A simple predictive "model" just predicts the most likely outcome within each bucket. Order matters: P(A∣B)≠P(B∣A)P(A \mid B) \ne P(B \mid A)P(A∣B)=P(B∣A) in general. Confusing the two is the base-rate fallacy. Bayes' theorem converts one into the other.

given

Probability of the conditioning event P(B)P(B)P(B). Pronounced: "the probability of B". The size of the bucket we condition on. Small buckets give noisy estimates: a conditional probability computed from 5 cases deserves far less trust than one computed from 5000.
