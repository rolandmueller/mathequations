Confidence Interval for the Mean

A confidence interval puts a margin of error around the sample mean. The margin is a critical value from the t distribution times the standard error: the spread of the data divided by the square root of the sample size. For large samples the t distribution is practically normal, so the normal critical value, 1.96 for a 95% interval, can be used instead. In words: point estimate plus or minus critical value times standard error.

Read aloud: the confidence interval is x bar plus or minus t star with n minus one degrees of freedom times s over the square root of n. For large n, it is approximately x bar plus or minus z star times s over the square root of n.

Definitions

ci

Confidence interval CI\mathrm{CI}CI. Pronounced: "the confidence interval". The correct reading is about the procedure: if we repeated the study many times, 95% of the intervals built this way would contain the true mean μ\muμ. Any single interval either contains μ\muμ or not. It is not "a 95% probability that μ\muμ lies in this particular interval". Conditions (ISRS 4.1): independent observations, and either n≥30n \ge 30n≥30 without extreme skew, or nearly normal data for smaller nnn. A simulation makes the coverage concrete: draw many samples, build an interval for each, and count how many cover μ\muμ. The bootstrap percentile interval is the assumption-light alternative.

mean

Sample mean xˉ\bar{x}xˉ. Pronounced: "x bar". The point estimate at the center of the interval.

crit

Critical value tn−1⋆t^{\star}_{n-1}tn−1⋆​. Pronounced: "t star with n minus one degrees of freedom". The quantile of the ttt distribution that leaves 2.5% in each tail for a 95% interval: scipy.stats.t.ppf(0.975, df=n-1)\texttt{scipy.stats.t.ppf(0.975, df=n-1)}scipy.stats.t.ppf(0.975, df=n-1). Use it whenever σ\sigmaσ is estimated, which is almost always; software does.

zcrit

Normal critical value z⋆z^{\star}z⋆. Pronounced: "z star". The quantile of the standard normal distribution that leaves 2.5% in each tail: z⋆=1.96z^{\star} = 1.96z⋆=1.96 for a 95% interval (scipy.stats.norm.ppf(0.975)\texttt{scipy.stats.norm.ppf(0.975)}scipy.stats.norm.ppf(0.975)). Other levels: 1.645 for 90%, 2.576 for 99%. The rule of thumb "mean plus or minus two standard errors" rounds 1.96 up to 2. Justified by the central limit theorem: for large nnn the sample mean is approximately normal, and sss is close enough to σ\sigmaσ that the ttt and normal quantiles agree (at n=100n = 100n=100, t⋆=1.98t^{\star} = 1.98t⋆=1.98). For small samples z⋆z^{\star}z⋆ gives intervals that are too narrow, covering μ\muμ less often than promised.

sd

Sample standard deviation sss. Pronounced: "s". The spread of individual observations.

root

Square root of the sample size, n\sqrt{n}n​. Pronounced: "root n". The n\sqrt{n}n​ law: to halve the width of the interval you need four times as much data. s/ns / \sqrt{n}s/n​ together is the standard error of the mean.
