Binomial Distribution

P(K=k)=(nk)pk(1−p)n−k\htmlClass{term-prob}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{P(K = k)}} = \htmlClass{term-binom}{\textcolor{#10b981}{\binom{n}{k}}} \htmlClass{term-success}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{p^{k}}} \htmlClass{term-failure}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{(1-p)^{n-k}}}P(K=k)=(kn​)pk(1−p)n−k

The probability of exactly k successes in n independent yes/no trials. Any one particular sequence with k successes has probability p to the k times the failure probability to the n − k, and there are n choose k such sequences.

Read aloud: the probability that K equals k equals n choose k times p to the k times one minus p to the n minus k.

Definitions

prob

Probability of kkk successes, P(K=k)P(K = k)P(K=k). Pronounced: "the probability that K equals k". KKK (capital) is the random number of successes; kkk (lowercase) is a specific value. The distribution has mean npnpnp and variance np(1−p)np(1-p)np(1−p). Examples: clicks among nnn visitors (A/B tests), heads in nnn coin flips, the number of up-steps in a random walk of nnn steps. In scipy: binom.pmf(k, n, p)\texttt{binom.pmf(k, n, p)}binom.pmf(k, n, p); to simulate: np.random.binomial(n, p, size)\texttt{np.random.binomial(n, p, size)}np.random.binomial(n, p, size).
