Gradient Descent

wt+1=wt−η ∇wL(wt)\htmlClass{term-new}{\textcolor{#8b5cf6}{w_{t+1}}} = \htmlClass{term-old}{\textcolor{#10b981}{w_t}} - \htmlClass{term-lr}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{\eta}} \, \htmlClass{term-grad}{\textcolor{#3b82f6}{\nabla_w L(w_t)}}wt+1​=wt​−η∇w​L(wt​)

Gradient descent finds good weights by walking downhill on the loss. Starting from the current weights, compute the gradient of the loss, which points uphill, and take a step of size eta in the opposite direction to get the new weights. Repeat until the loss stops improving.

Read aloud: w t plus one equals w t minus eta times the gradient of L with respect to w at w t.

Definitions

new

Updated weights wt+1w_{t+1}wt+1​. Pronounced: "w t plus one". www stands for all parameters of the model at once (every weight and bias in a network), a vector with possibly millions of entries.

old

Current weights wtw_twt​. Pronounced: "w t". ttt counts update steps. Networks start from small random weights, so two training runs can end in different places.

lr

Learning rate η\etaη. Pronounced: "eta". The most important tuning knob. Too large and the loss bounces around or explodes; too small and training crawls. Typical starting values are 0.001 to 0.01. Optimizers like Adam adapt the step size per weight (optimizer=keras.optimizers.Adam(learning_rate=1e-3)\texttt{optimizer=keras.optimizers.Adam(learning\_rate=1e-3)}optimizer=keras.optimizers.Adam(learning_rate=1e-3)).

grad

Gradient of the loss ∇wL(wt)\nabla_w L(w_t)∇w​L(wt​). Pronounced: "the gradient of L with respect to w at w t". The symbol ∇\nabla∇ is "nabla" or "del". The vector of partial derivatives ∂L∂w\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}∂w∂L​: how much the loss changes when each weight is nudged. In a network it is computed layer by layer with the chain rule (backpropagation). Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) estimates it from a small random minibatch rather than all the data: noisier but much faster. One pass through all minibatches is an epoch.
