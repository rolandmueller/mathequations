LIME (Local Surrogate Model)

LIME explains one prediction of a black-box model f by fitting a simple model around that single point. The explanation is the simple model, chosen from an interpretable family, that best balances two goals: mimic the black box near x, and stay simple enough to read.

Read aloud: xi of x equals the g in G that minimizes L of f, g and pi x plus omega of g.

Definitions

expl

Explanation ξ(x)\xi(x)ξ(x). Pronounced: "xi of x". A local surrogate: typically a sparse linear model whose coefficients say which features pushed this prediction up or down. Recipe: perturb xxx many times, ask the black box to predict each perturbation, weight the samples by closeness to xxx, and fit a weighted, sparse linear model. The lime\texttt{lime}lime package implements it for tables, text, and images.

argmin

Best interpretable model arg⁡min⁡g∈G\arg\min_{g \in G}argming∈G​. Pronounced: "the g in G that minimizes". GGG is the family of interpretable models, e.g. linear models with at most 5 features or short trees.

loss

Fidelity loss L(f,g,πx)L(f, g, \pi_x)L(f,g,πx​). Pronounced: "L of f, g and pi x". How badly ggg approximates the black box fff, usually a weighted squared error over the perturbed samples. πx\pi_xπx​ is a proximity kernel: samples close to xxx get large weights. The kernel width is the weak spot: different widths can give different, even contradictory, explanations, and explanations of nearby points can be unstable (Interpretable ML book, LIME chapter).

complex

Complexity penalty Ω(g)\Omega(g)Ω(g). Pronounced: "omega of g". E.g. the number of features used. In practice the user fixes it directly, for example "use 5 features".
