Multiple Linear Regression

Multiple regression predicts the response from several predictors at once: an intercept plus, for each of the p predictors, its own coefficient times its value, plus an error term. Each coefficient is the association of one predictor with the response while holding all others fixed, which is how regression adjusts for confounders.

Read aloud: Y equals beta zero plus the sum from j equals one to p of beta j times X j, plus epsilon.

This is why a coefficient can change sign when a variable is added: in the beer-and-chips example, the simple slope mixes the effect of one product with that of a correlated one, and adding the confounder separates them. Strongly correlated predictors (collinearity) make individual coefficients unstable, with large standard errors, even when predictions are fine.
